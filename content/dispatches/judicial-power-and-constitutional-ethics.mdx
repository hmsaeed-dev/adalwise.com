## The Autonomy of the Qadi

In the classical Islamic constitutional framework, the judiciary (*al-Qada'*) operates not as an executive instrument of the Sultan, but as an independent guardian of Divine and customary justice (*al-Haqq*).

> **Historic Precedent:**
> The famous letter of Caliph Umar ibn al-Khattab to Abu Musa al-Ash'ari stands as the cornerstone of judicial impartiality:
> *"Jurisprudence is an established obligation and an enacted tradition. Understand thoroughly when a dispute is brought before you, for speaking truth without execution is futile."*

## The Three Safeguards of Judicial Integrity

1. **Immunity from Executive Dismissal for Verdicts**: A judge could not be removed merely for rendering a judgment adverse to the treasury or executive apparatus.
2. **Public Scrutiny in the Mosque/Chamber**: Court sessions were accessible to the common citizenry, preventing clandestine deliberations.
3. **Subjection of the Head of State to Subpoena**: Canonical accounts document caliphs standing alongside ordinary plaintiffs in court without superior ceremonial seating.
