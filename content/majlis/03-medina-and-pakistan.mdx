On Sunday, 20 September 2026, Adlwise convened **Majlis 03** at **Canal View Farms, Manhala Road, Lahore**. Conceived under the banner **"A Youth Dialogue & Day Together"** (*نوجوانوں کے ساتھ مکالمہ اور ایک مشترکہ دن*), this gathering departed from the conventional lecture format, uniting scholars, jurists, university students, and young professionals in an immersive six-hour day retreat.

The assembly integrated unhurried communal fraternity—beginning with morning arrival and traditional dastarkhwan dining, informal poolside brotherhood, and culminating in a rigorous recorded symposium panel deliberating the statecraft of Madinah and its constitutional relevance for contemporary Pakistan.

---

## Thematic Premise: Covenantal Federalism vs. Modern Executive Despotism

Pakistan’s contemporary constitutional impasse is frequently framed as an irresolvable binary between secular proceduralism and autocratic clericalism. In reality, modern bureaucratic state centralization has hollowed out both Islamic covenantal ethics and genuine civil liberty.

Majlis 03 returned to primary constitutional history—specifically the **Mithaq al-Madina** (Charter of Medina, 622 CE)—to examine how the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ constructed a constitutional commonwealth that was simultaneously:

1. **Pluralistic and Multi-Confessional:** Recognising diverse confessional communities (Muslims, Jewish tribes, and allied polytheists) as autonomous legal jurisdictions (*Ummah wahidah*) bound by reciprocal defense and civic peace obligations.
2. **Decentralized and Covenantal:** Delegating civil disputes, blood-money administration, and welfare to organic local guilds and tribes, rather than a monolithic surveillance bureaucracy.
3. **Founded on Substantive Justice (*Adl*):** Establishing that political authority derives its legitimacy entirely from covenantal fidelity, judicial impartiality, and the protection of natural rights.

---

## Anatomy of the Day: The Retreat Experience

True intellectual renewal (*Ihya*) requires an atmosphere free from corporate pretense. Majlis 03 was structured to revive the classical Islamic practice of **Suhbah** (learned companionship and discipleship), where the teacher and student share bread before debating philosophy.

### I. 10:00 AM — Morning Arrival & Dastarkhwan Breakfast
Participants arrived at Canal View Farms off the Lahore Canal and Manhala Road. The morning commenced around a single woven mat (*dastarkhwan*), sharing warm Lahori kulchas, freshly cooked chanay, curd, seeds, and morning tea. Sitting side-by-side on the floor set an immediate tone of humility, mutual trust, and egalitarian brotherhood.

### II. 11:30 AM — Poolside Fraternity & Open Dialogue
Before formal lectures, participants spent the late morning outdoors in the farmhouse courtyard and swimming pool. This unstructured time broke the ice between senior scholars and young attendees, allowing honest conversations on personal faith crises, career anxieties, and questions about Pakistan's future.

### III. 1:00 PM — The Symposium Deliberation
The gathering assembled indoors in the main hall for the formal symposium. With a four-person panel equipped with wireless lapel microphones and multi-angle video recording, Dr. Hafiz Haseeb delivered the keynote exposition on *Manhaj-e-Inqilab-e-Nabawi ﷺ*, followed by responses from constitutional lawyers and public policy fellows. An intense, participatory Q&A followed, covering historical treaty clauses, the Objectives Resolution (1949), and municipal decentralization.

### IV. 2:45 PM — Communal Feast & Post-Session Fellowship
Following the formal proceedings, attendees gathered once again for lunch around the dastarkhwan—sharing platters of biryani, raita, salad, and cold refreshments. Conversations continued spontaneously, clarifying legal nuances and exchanging book recommendations.

### V. 3:45 PM — Resolutions, Group Assembly & Dua
The session concluded with the formal assembly portrait, distribution of digital working papers and slide decks, and a collective prayer for the spiritual and institutional revival of Pakistan.
